April 8, 2026

I am booked every day from 7 am to 9pm, which leaves little time for projects, fun or hobbies. I am almost religious about my need to go to the gym and move my body. When I am having a particularly rough day I find myself lacking in endorphins and start walking to the west end of campus. The lengths I will go to to make sure this activity fits in my day, includes planning my schedule around it, packing gym clothes (making my side bag almost 40 lbs), and even changing my route to class.

I think being able to move my body helps center me as a small dot in a big universe. It helps me refocus on the important things after a particularly stressful day and it is a guaranteed window of time I am not on devices and I feel its acceptable to not respond to the overwhelming number of messages and emails. Running for example helps me feel small and free, doing an exercise every human has done before me feels shrinking.

I have even begun to tie pay incentives and work experience with my enjoyment of exercising. I have always been a part of a team, wether it was my high school sports team, my club team or even my family tennis team, I knew that working towards a physical goal is something I crave. When I decided to come to WashU I made the decision to not swim on the varsity swim team here. When I came for school in the fall, I knew I wanted to still be involved in the fitness community. I am a Nationally Academy of Sports Medicine Certified Group Trainer and I currently teach spin and strength classes here at Washu. It is hard to describe the amount of enjoyment and contentment I get from just teaching my class. I teach every week from 12-1 on fridays, and I can tell you it is the thing I look forward to the most, even more than the weekend at the end of a long week. I have been taught so many lessons from this job and I am grateful for the platform to make a small difference in people's every day life.
