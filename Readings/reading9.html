April 13, 2026

A human-made creation that brings out the best in me is literature. Books feel pure to me because they allow me to read the thoughts, stories, memories, and imaginations of other people. Some books are rooted in real life, while others are completely invented, but both kinds make me think more broadly and deeply. Reading gives me a way to spend time intentionally learning, reflecting, and entering into perspectives that are not my own.

Literature brings out my curiosity, empathy, and patience. It reminds me that there is always more to notice and more to understand, and it gives me the space to slow down instead of rushing through everything. I also love that reading can happen wherever I am, which makes it feel rich and personal. Whether I am reading for a class or for myself, books help me feel more thoughtful, grounded, and open to the world around me. My favorite book is actually about death: Tuesdays with Morrie. It is about learning how to die, but also about learning how to live with no regrets. The book follows lessons that happen every Tuesday between a professor and his former college student, and each lesson focuses on the most important parts of life. I feel like I can reread it at any point in my life and think different things, reflect in new ways, and learn something every time. It feels new each time I read it, but also like returning to an old friend at the same time. That makes it incredibly special to me.

“Death ends a life, not a relationship.” — Mitch Albom, Tuesdays with Morrie
