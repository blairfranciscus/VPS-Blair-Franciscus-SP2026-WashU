Writing Response 4

April 20, 2026

Something I appreciate and long for from the past is being able to go to my grandma's cabin in the Colorado woods. The cabin had no Wi-Fi and no service, and I think that is exactly what made it feel so peaceful. I miss how easy it used to feel to unplug there. Now, so much of my life is decided by what my past self puts into my calendar, and it can feel like every hour already belongs to something. At the cabin, time felt more open. I was not constantly checking messages, missing emails, or worrying about whether someone needed me to respond right away.

My family has a new cabin now on my grandma's dream lot by the lake she lived by. It is not the old cabin built by my grandpa, who has passed away, but one she built herself. It is beautiful, modern, and still an amazing place to go, but it has Wi-Fi, and more of the surrounding area has service now too. That changes the feeling of retreating there. Even when I want to unplug, the ability to stay connected almost forces me to live in the future instead of the present. It feels like my past self plans my present, and my present self is always planning my future, but neither version of me gets to fully choose a future freely or sit still long enough to focus on the present.

I also miss the way my family could retreat there together. We would play with wood, play outside, and play games without the pressure of jobs, responsibilities, or constant availability. There was a kind of freedom in being unreachable. It taught me that living well sometimes means stepping outside of systems that expect us to always be useful, responsive, and connected. The wisdom of that place was simple: rest is easier when no one can reach you, and presence becomes deeper when there is nothing pulling you away from where you are.
