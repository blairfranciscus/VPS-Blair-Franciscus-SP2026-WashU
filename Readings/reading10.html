April 15, 2026

I cherish the slow or hard way of doing something when I know it is going to change me, not just get me to the finish line faster. I think there is something really meaningful about having to learn how to be good at something. Most of the time, that process is not a choice. It is just what happens when you care about a goal enough to keep trying even when you are not good yet. Failing at something before really learning it can be frustrating, but it also feels like the only honest way to grow. If something is handed to me too easily, I might still appreciate it, but I do not feel shaped by it in the same way.

Swimming taught me this more than almost anything else. As a competitive swimmer, I worked toward goals that took time, repetition, and a lot of discomfort. The sport hurt, but that was also what made the accomplishments feel real. When I finally reached a goal after months of practice, it felt completely different than if someone had simply given me the result. The slower, harder path gave the achievement meaning because I knew what it had cost. That kind of organic growth toward a goal brings out discipline, patience, and confidence in me. It reminds me that fulfillment does not always come from convenience. Sometimes it comes from effort, limits, and the feeling that you earned the person you became along the way.
