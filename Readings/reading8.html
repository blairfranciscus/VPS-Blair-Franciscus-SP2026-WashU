Do Artifacts Have Ethics?

April 11, 2026

I think this argument about whether artifacts have ethics is especially relevant today, as humans continue to develop technology with the power to do incredible things, like sending people to the moon, but also the ability to destroy entire countries in seconds. The article really highlights how technology is never neutral; it always carries moral weight, whether we recognize it or not.

I especially appreciated the questions it raises, like how technology affects our experience of time, what it encourages us to ignore, and what risks it creates for others. These feel incredibly real in everyday life. For example, with scrolling or watching TV, technology does not actually make time pass faster. It just pulls us in and distracts us from noticing it. If it truly sped up time, we would not be so drawn to it.

The idea of what technology makes us ignore is also pretty powerful. While it enhances certain aspects of life, it can mute others, like nature, real human connection, or simply being present. It can make us forget that we are human and that we belong outside of our screens.

Finally, the question of consent really stands out to me. So much technology impacts people who never agreed to it, whether it is weapons, surveillance, or even being recorded without permission. These are not small issues. They are shaping the world in major ways.

Overall, this article pushes us to ask more intentional questions about the role technology plays in our lives, especially as it continues to evolve so quickly.
